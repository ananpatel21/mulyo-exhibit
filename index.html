    Take-home value cards

    One 4 × 6 in card per value, stacked in a rack at the Diya Wall. Visitors take the card for the value they lit. Each carries a one-line story and one small thing to try this week.

    More ideas to layer in

    Flow

    Mulyo Passport

    A fold card with eight boxes, handed out at the welcome wall. One self-inking stamp per station; a finished card earns a diya sticker at the exit. It keeps families moving in order.

    Media

    QR story videos

    Every panel gets a QR code to its 30-second story video. Visitors watch on their phones, so the only screens needed are the Aadar video and one story TV in the south aisle.

    Kids

    Mushak hunt

    Hide a small Mushak (Ganeshji's mouse) at each station. Kids count them and tell the volunteer at the Diya Wall for a small prize.

    Santosh

    Gratitude counter

    A small chalkboard by the wall: “Leaves added today: ___”, updated each hour. People like seeing the number climb.

    Kshama

    Dissolving-paper option

    Alongside the backpack, visitors write a grudge on water-soluble paper and drop it in a bowl of water. It disappears in seconds.

    Vivek

    Kids' crossword

    Print a letter-size version of the word search on the back of the passport, so younger visitors can finish it while parents read panels.

    Tyag

    Thank-you cards

    Add a small ledge with cards so visitors can write to the crews who lent the gear. Deliver them, with a signed banner, after Diwali.

    Entrance

    Rangoli threshold

    A walk-on rangoli vinyl at the entrance from Festival marks the shift from celebration to values, and gives a natural photo moment.

    Notes before you build

    • Aisles. Each corridor is 10 ft; with 2.5 ft tables you keep about 7 ft clear, enough for two-way traffic and strollers.
    • Spine wall. The 32 ft center wall carries panels on both faces. Confirm whether it can be a hard wall or double-sided pipe-and-drape with panels on frames, and that the south face has power for its story TV. The north wall needs power for the Aadar screen. The north face now carries the 25 ft Ramayan mural.
    • Values chosen. The eight here match the packet's station ideas. Satya/Nishtha, Ahimsa and Shuddhi are left out; the packet's Shuddhi story repeats the Ahimsa story and p. 5 still has template text, so fix those first if you add them.
    • Section 3 overlap. Several stories (Shiv, Ganeshji, Ram) may also appear in the deity section. Agree on who tells which story before artwork.
    • Lighting. Gym overheads stay off (keep exit signs and any required egress lighting on). About 20 clamp-on LED spots on the wall tops, warm white around 3000K, each aimed at one panel, except Santosh, which uses UV floods so the glow tree reads; 3 floor uplights on the kavad, gear mannequin and mirror plinth; the Aadar screen lights its own bench; the Diya Wall glows on its own tealights. The orange visitor path can be glow tape or low-profile LED strip under a clear cover. Check outlets and circuits with the venue, and put the spots on dimmers. Use “Exhibit lighting” in the viewer to compare with house lights.
    • Value signs. Each value’s banner is an 8 × 1.7 ft header mounted on top of the wall directly above its station, from about 8.25 to 10 ft, so it reads from across the room. Mount them on drape-pipe extensions or the spine’s frame, and light them with the station spots.
    • Volunteers. Plan one guide per aisle to start activities, wipe the word search, and reload the backpacks and kavad baskets.
